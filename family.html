Family Legacy In Others' Words
01

Family

Grounded
by family.

Behind the public role, Sulayman credits his wife and family for keeping him grounded through the demands of leading a state agency, and strives to make time for family and for his own well-being.

Balancing leadership with personal responsibilities has been challenging. However, the unrelenting support of my wife and family keeps me grounded, while I strive to create time for self-care and family commitments.

Interview, NiCare Newsletter Vol. 1, 2025

He studied in the United Kingdom, at the University of East London and Coventry University, before building his career in Nigeria's public health system, first at the National Primary Health Care Development Agency in Abuja and now in Niger State, where he lives in Minna.

02

A Legacy in the Making

Institutions do not outperform the capacity of the people who drive them.

Letter from the Executive Secretary, 2026
I

Coverage for those most at risk

Coverage for vulnerable persons grew from 3,816 to 26,155 in two years, with new schemes for retirees, inmates, students, women and children, and a drive to enrol 65,000 more vulnerable residents.

II

An institution built to last

A restructured Agency with revised Operational Guidelines, claims SOPs, stronger internal controls, an M&E system and ICT governance: foundations designed to outlive any one tenure.

III

People developed

Targeted capacity building for staff, equal opportunity and the first NiCare Productivity Awards to recognise excellence, including pioneer and retired staff.

IV

Health insurance, owned locally

Traditional and religious leaders, Ward Development Committees, political Health Insurance Drive Champions and the First Lady as Ambassador, so that coverage is locally driven and socially embraced.

Over the next few years, I envision NiCare as a state-of-the-art agency of international repute, leading the charge in universal health coverage with groundbreaking initiatives.

Interview, NiCare Newsletter Vol. 1, 2025
