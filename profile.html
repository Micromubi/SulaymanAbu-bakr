About

Sulayman
Abu-Bakr, MPH

Health financing and digital health leader, working to make quality healthcare affordable for every family in Niger State.

Sulayman Abu-Bakr is a public health practitioner and programme manager whose family roots lie in Lapai, Niger State, and whose work sits where health policy meets data.

He trained first in technology, earning a BSc in Business Information Systems at the University of East London, before taking a Master of Public Health with Distinction at Nile University of Nigeria.

At the National Primary Health Care Development Agency in Abuja, which he joined in 2014, he helped build the digital backbone of Nigeria's primary health care system: deploying DHIS2, leading the Electronic Management of Immunization Data (EMID) work, supporting the COVID-19 Crisis Management Centre, and serving as a US CDC GEEKS Fellow who managed the national DHIS2 server.

In January 2024 he became Executive Secretary and CEO of the Niger State Contributory Health Agency. In two years enrolment grew by 83.3%, from [baseline figure] to [current figure]; coverage for vulnerable residents rose roughly sevenfold, from [from] to [to]; and the Agency was recognised nationally among the five best performing state social health insurance agencies in a feature in The Guardian Weekend (13–14 September 2025). He introduced and oversees the Agency's digital systems: NiFMS, e-Claims, e-Voucher and AI-supported enrolee tracking.

He chairs the Niger State Health Financing Technical Working Group and serves on several state and national health committees. In 2026 he founded the Sule Lapai Foundation, named after his grandfather, one of Niger State's pioneer Permanent Secretaries. He credits his wife and family for keeping him grounded.

Universal Health Coverage, to me, has always been a political decision before it is a technical one.
